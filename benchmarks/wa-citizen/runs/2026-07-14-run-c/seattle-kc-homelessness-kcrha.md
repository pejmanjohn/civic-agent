**Conclusion:** There is no single official number for "all homelessness spending" by Seattle and King County — but the best-supported picture is this: since 2022, most of both governments' homelessness money flows through the King County Regional Homelessness Authority (KCRHA), the joint city–county agency, which actually spent about $500 million from its 2021 startup through 2024 ($191.6M in 2024 alone). Seattle's own approved budget shows roughly $165M for homelessness programs in FY2026, about $127M of which is its payment to KCRHA. King County's side cannot be isolated from its published dashboard.

**Numbers:**
- KCRHA actual expenditures (filed with the State Auditor): 2021 $3.0M (startup year), 2022 $132.5M, 2023 $172.6M, 2024 $191.6M — cumulative 2021–2024 ≈ $499.8M. KCRHA ran a deficit in 2024 ($191.6M spent vs $180.7M revenues). 2025 not yet filed.
- Seattle approved operating budget, homelessness-named programs (Human Services Dept): FY2026 ≈ $164.8M total — KCRHA contribution $126.8M, City-Managed Homelessness Programs $38.1M, HOPE Team ≈ $0 (net offset row). Earlier years: FY2024 ≈ $113.6M, FY2022 ≈ $118.2M, FY2018 ≈ $32.0M (pre-KCRHA, program structure changed).
- King County: the Open Budget Dashboard only publishes department-level budgeted figures. DCHS (Community and Human Services), which houses homelessness work, has a FY2026 budgeted expenditure of $1.624B — but that covers far more than homelessness, so no county homelessness figure can be claimed.

**How to read this:** These are three different frames that must not be added together. (1) KCRHA figures are audited actual spending by the joint authority — the closest thing to "what was actually spent" — but they include money that originated as Seattle and King County contributions, so adding Seattle's KCRHA payment to KCRHA's spending double-counts. (2) Seattle figures are approved budget, not actual spending. (3) "Where did it go" at the sub-program level (shelters, outreach providers, vendors) is not answerable from these sources — KCRHA's filed actuals have no vendor or program detail, and neither city nor county publishes an accepted homelessness checkbook here. Answer mode: **partial** — the joint-authority actuals and Seattle's budget frame are solid; a county-side homelessness figure and provider-level detail are missing. The path for the missing pieces: KCRHA's published budgets/reports (kcrha.org) and King County DCHS budget documents.
